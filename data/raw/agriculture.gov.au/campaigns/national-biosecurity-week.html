Celebrate our biosecurity system

Biosecurity works quietly in the background, around the clock, protecting many things we all value. It helps safeguard the food we eat, the natural places we cherish, and the health of our plants, animals and communities.

National Biosecurity Week is our opportunity each year to draw back the curtain. We invite you to take a closer look at biosecurity in Australia.

Why biosecurity matters

A strong, modern biosecurity system is crucial for our nation’s success.

Our farms, fisheries and produce around $100 billion in agricultural products each year and approximately $70 billion in premium products for export.

Biosecurity helps to:

  • grow our economy
  • open international markets
  • protect our food, environment, health and jobs.

Strong animal biosecurity also helps protect Australia from harmful pests and diseases, and maintains our strong animal, plant and human health reputation.

Protecting animal health helps support healthier communities and ecosystems.

How biosecurity works

Pests and diseases can cause serious damage to our plants, animals and natural environment.

Australia is free from many of the world’s worst pest and diseases. If these exotic pests and diseases were to enter our country, they would destroy our crops and devastate our livestock and poultry.

A strong biosecurity system helps prevent these pests and diseases from entering and establishing in Australia.

Working pre-border, at the border and beyond the border, Australia’s biosecurity system provides multiple layers of protection and helps us respond quickly if risks are detected.

What we do

We work with communities, industries, organisations and governments in Australia and overseas to strengthen our biosecurity system.

We continue to enhance and widen our biosecurity operations by:

  • sharing data and intelligence with trusted national and international agencies
  • increasing surveillance for exotic pests and diseases across our region
  • expanding offshore treatment activities.

Australia’s Chief Veterinary Officer, Chief Plant Protection Officer and Chief Environmental Biosecurity Officer play a crucial role in our biosecurity system. Together, they provide trusted advice, champion a One Health approach, and guide responses to plant, animal and environmental risks.

Hear from our Three Chiefs about their roles and how they work together to protect Australia.

How you can help

Biosecurity isn’t just about border checks - it’s a shared responsibility across individuals, businesses and governments. Whether you're a farmer, a traveller, a scientist, or interested in protecting your way of life, everyone has a role to play.

See it. Record it. Report it.
