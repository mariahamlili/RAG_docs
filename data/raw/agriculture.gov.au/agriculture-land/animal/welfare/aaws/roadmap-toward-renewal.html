Animal group chapters

The AAWS will include all animals. Each chapter will be dedicated to one of 6 animal groups, with the strategy expected to be finalised in 2027:

This chapter includes:

  • Animals in production systems, including livestock such as terrestrial mammals, poultry and crocodiles.

This chapter includes:

  • Aquatic animals commercially farmed or harvested in their own environment
  • Aquatic animals caught or used in recreation activities (recreational fishing, the ornamental/aquarium industry).

This chapter includes:

  • Native animals, including marine fauna
  • Introduced and feral animals, including feral animals harvested in the wild
  • Native animal management, including animals that are harvested in their own environment
  • Marine animal management, excluding animals that are harvested in their own environment
  • Native wildlife parks and rescues
  • The conservation and scientific study of native animals in captivity.

This chapter includes:

  • All animals that are kept as pets, including dogs, cats, rabbits, fish, birds, amphibians, reptiles, ferrets and rodents
  • Breeding and behavioural training of companion animals
  • Horses, donkeys and mules, poultry and other animals typically used in production systems kept on hobby farms or as pets.

This chapter includes:

  • Animals used for work (e.g. service animals, support animals of any species, muster dogs)
  • Animals used in sport (e.g. horse racing, greyhound racing, equestrian events)
  • Animals used in recreation (e.g. circuses, magicians, rodeos, zoos, dog events)
  • Animals involved in recreational hunting.

This chapter includes:

  • All animals used in research and teaching.

The parameters for each chapter have been developed based on feedback from stakeholders and consideration of the previous AAWS. Many animal welfare considerations are relevant to multiple species, chapters and stakeholder groups.

Priority work streams

The overarching strategy is supported by the following 6 priority work streams that provide a consistent framework across every chapter:

  • Leadership and coordination
  • Standards and implementation
  • Reporting and compliance
  • Research and development
  • Education and communication
  • International engagement

These priority work streams:

  • build on the goals of the previous strategy
  • were agreed during the first round of consultation
  • are now embedded in the National Statement on Animal Welfare.

Goals and objectives for each work stream will be included in the final AAWS.

How the priority work streams, goals and objectives and chapters work together

AAWS overarching strategy documentAnimal group chapters

Draws on the National Statement on Animal Welfare to include a vision, context setting and the 6 priority work streams outlined above.

This document will also identify goals and objectives aligned to each priority work stream.

Each chapter is dedicated to 1 of 6 animal groups.

  • livestock and production animals
  • aquatic animals
  • animals in the wild
  • companion animal
  • animals used for work, sport and recreation
  • animals used in research and teaching.

Each animal group chapter will provide a snapshot of the current sector’s environment, including achievements and challenges, and identify opportunities for continuous improvement.
