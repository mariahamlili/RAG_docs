Joint Agriculture Ministers' Meeting and Environment Ministers' Meeting HPAI H5 update

Meeting communiqué

31 July 2026

Commonwealth, state, and territory agriculture and environment ministers met on 31 July 2026, for the first joint Agriculture Ministers’ Meeting (AMM) and Environment Ministers’ Meeting (EMM) to discuss the evolving H5 bird flu situation.

This meeting was jointly chaired by the Australian Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry, Minister Julie Collins and Minister for the Environment and Water, Senator the Hon Murray Watt.

H5 bird flu situation update

Agriculture and environment ministers met jointly to discuss Australia's preparedness for and response to H5 bird flu, recognising the importance of a coordinated approach across governments and sectors for coordinated decision-making and response efforts.

Ministers received an update from expert officials on the current H5 bird flu situation in Australia and noted that jurisdictions are leading on-ground response activities, supported by national coordination arrangements.

Officials briefed the group on the decision to enact the H5 National Management Agreement based on advice from the Consultative Committee on Emergency Animal Diseases (CCEAD) that H5 bird flu in wildlife is not technically feasible to eradicate in wildlife following recent detection and evidence of local transmission of H5 bird flu in a small number of wild seabirds.

Ministers noted the activation of the National Management Agreement for H5 HPAI in wildlife and welcomed the close partnership between agriculture and environment agencies in implementing the agreement.

In addition to the $113 million in funding already committed by the Australian Government to strengthen our nation's preparedness for H5 bird flu, Ministers noted that on 21 July 2026, the National Management Group (NMG) committed to cost-share up to $24.8 million to support response activities undertaken since the first confirmed detections of H5 bird flu in Australia.

Ministers committed to continue working closely as we adapt to the evolving situation, noting that planning has been undertaken for all credible scenarios, and Ministers reaffirmed the importance of ongoing readiness.

Ministers recognised the importance of nationally coordinated communications in supporting public confidence, promoting awareness, and ensuring stakeholders receive clear, consistent, and timely information.

Ministers reaffirmed their commitment to continued collaboration across governments, industries, and sectors to protect Australia's wildlife, agricultural industries, communities, and economy from the impacts of H5 bird flu.
