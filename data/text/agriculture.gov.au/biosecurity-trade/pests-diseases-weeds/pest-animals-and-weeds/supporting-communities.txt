We are building on past investment to find better solutions for land managers to control and reduce the impact of established non-native pest animals and weeds.
The program is:
- working with States and Territories to deliver on-ground management
- supporting Indigenous Ranger Groups in Northern Australia to undertake on-ground pest and weed management
- supporting actions identified under the Threatened Species Action Plan through natural resource management organisations
- funding research and development to advance a range of new control methods
- supporting national coordination to improve information sharing, coordination and adoption of best practice management
- improving data, to better our understanding of their impact and distribution
Funding
From 2021–22 to 2024–25, the program received a total of $49.1 million in funding.
In the 2025–26 Budget, the Australian Government committed an additional $11 million to continue national efforts targeting feral deer, pigs, foxes, and cats. This funding also supports Indigenous-led pest and weed management in northern Australia, research to improve control tools, and joint initiatives with state and territory governments to reduce and prevent pest animals and weeds.
Funding allocations were made in consultation with states and territories and in line with government financial guidelines. Program priorities are guided by national strategies and action plans.
Projects
In March 2022, then Minister for Agriculture and Northern Australia, and then Minister for the Environment, approved $20 million for 30 state and territory-led projects (Tranche 2). These Tranche 2 projects are delivering on-ground management of established pest and weeds to reduce the impact on our agricultural production, our native wildlife and environment, and the community.
In July 2025, the Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry approved $5 million for 26 state and territory-led projects (Tranche 3) to continue this work.
Our funding in Tranche 2 and Tranche 3 leverages over $50 million from state and territory governments.

Projects
Novel rat-specific toxin for improved management of rats in intensive livestock production systems
Delivered by: CSIRO, partnering with Australian Pork Limited, Poultry Hub, and Landcare Research NZ.
Current control strategies rely mainly on anticoagulant rodenticides, which can negatively impact non-target species. This project will assess the effectiveness and delivery of a newly designed analogue of the rat specific chemical toxin, Norbormide, in Australian Intensive Livestock Production Systems. This project aims to deliver cost-effective rodent control that significantly reduces non-target risks to livestock and native wildlife.
Development of drone detection technology to enhance Tropical Soda Apple control in rugged high-value grazing country in northern NSW
Delivered by: North Coast Local Land Services, partnering with Charles Sturt University, Southern Cross University, Kempsey Shire Council, Coffs Harbour City Council, Clarence Valley Council and Rous County Council.
Tropical Soda Apple (TSA) is native to South America but highly invasive in agricultural and natural landscapes in Australia. Current control and management strategies are proving effective in accessible and easy to monitor environments such as open pastures. However, present management efforts are at risk of failure as TSA is difficult to control in rugged ‘out of sight’ terrain. This project aims to develop drone-based remote sensing technology to detect TSA. This will allow targeted control activities that will greatly increase the chances for regional-scale eradication.
Night-Adapted Eye survey and species classification system — realising the capacity for real-time and cost-effective monitoring of pests on a landscape scale
Delivered by: Monash University, partnering with Nocturnal Wildlife Research Pty. Ltd., Definium Technologies Pty. Ltd., Firmus Grid Pty. Ltd., University of Southern Queensland, Zoophotonics.
Current pest survey methods are mostly highly labour intensive, non-automated, and require the placement of devices in the field that need to be maintained. This project aims to utilise the power of high-performance computing and Artificial Intelligence to develop a hand-held digital sensor that can determine the distribution, density, and species classification of pests in many hectares of habitat in minutes.
Integrated chemical and biological control systems for established agricultural and environmental weeds
Delivered by: The University of Queensland, partnering with CSIRO, and Bayer AG.
The main chemical control tools deployed by farmers are either herbicides or biological control agents such as insects or fungal pathogens. Herbicides are often not entirely effective and can negatively impact non-target species including biological control agents. The goal of this project is to modify existing herbicides to become more effective, and to develop new herbicides that target an important plant enzyme not found in animals that allows for easier integration of chemical and biological control systems.
A further $3 million has been provided for the following projects:
National Rabbit Disease Monitoring Program
Delivered by: CSIRO
The National Rabbit Disease Monitoring Program provides critical data on the various rabbit viruses circulating in Australia, to inform potential future rabbit biocontrol agents. It extends rabbit monitoring and diagnostic services, offering free testing services to community members to diagnose rabbit viruses in rabbits and hares that are found dead. In addition, CSIRO will select a representative sample to keep track of any new virus variants, new virus recombinants, and monitor general trends in viral variance and evolution. This investment builds on previous Commonwealth-supported projects managed by the Centre for Invasive Species Solutions (CISS).
Further information on the program can be found at CSIRO. Further information on rabbit biocontrol in Australia can be found at PestSmart.
Year 1 of Phase I – Future Proofing Rabbit Biocontrol
Delivered by: CSIRO
This contract provides funding to CSIRO to commence Phase 1 of a Future Proofing Rabbit Biocontrol program, which aims to produce Australia’s next rabbit biocontrol virus using natural selection processes to obtain new Rabbit Haemorrhagic Disease Virus (RHDV) variants as candidates for future virus releases.
Resulting virus variants that overcome immunity to existing virus strains can be further assessed as potential candidates for registration and release as rabbit biocontrol agents.
Further information on rabbit biocontrol in Australia can be found at PestSmart
Community engagement in release of biocontrol agents
Delivered by: CSIRO
This project will undertake classical weed biocontrol for Spinyhead sida (Sida acuta, Phase III, mass rearing and release) and sea spurge (Euphorbia paralias, Phase III, mass rearing and release), which were identified as national biocontrol priorities. Activities will aim to advance biocontrol research and practice for these two species along the weed biocontrol pipeline over a two-year period, at which point their outcomes will be evaluated.
Siam weed biocontrol
Delivered by: Department of Primary Industries Queensland
This project will progress nationally identified research activities in the biological control of Siam Weed (Chromolaena odorata) in northern Australia. The project will undertake host specificity testing of a potential new biocontrol agent (the defoliating Erebid moth, Pareuchaetes pseudoinsulata). The project will also monitor the spread of Siam weed and the existing biocontrol agent (stem-galling fly, Cecidochares connexa) in north Queensland and the Northern Territory, through detailed surveys and mapping and provide training to Northern Territory field staff for monitoring distribution and impact of the gall fly.
Gorse biocontrol
Delivered by: Department of Energy, Environment and Climate Action Victoria
This project will progress nationally identified research activities in the biological control of Gorse (Ulex europaeus). The project will identify areas to search for new gorse biocontrol agents by assessing, mapping and matching native and invasive populations of gorse. This project aims to complete genetic analysis of gorse populations in Australia and its native range in western Europe; engage with community stakeholders in monitoring and collecting samples; establish a framework for effective biocontrol agent selection; and providing recommendations for the search for new biocontrol agents in the native range based on genetic and climatic compatibility.
Giant reed biocontrol
Delivered by: Department of Primary Industries and Regional Development New South Wales
This project will initiate foundational biocontrol research on giant reed (Arundo donax) in Australia. Population genetics, including number and origin of potential introductions into Australia, will be explored. Ecoclimatic models and risk assessments for two potential biocontrol agents (the shoot tip-galling wasp, T. romana and the armoured scale, R. donacis) will be developed to understand potential distribution. A test plant list and preliminary host-specificity testing of the shoot tip-galling wasp and armoured scale will be developed in Australian quarantine on key native and agriculturally significant species.

Projects
National Feral Pig Management Coordination Program
Delivered by: Australian Pork Limited
This grant provides co-investment to support delivery of the National Feral Pig Action Plan 2021 – 2031 which seeks to reduce impacts by protecting assets through the delivery of effective, coordinated, sustained and humane best practice management to suppress, or eradicate (where feasible), feral pig populations.
This includes supporting cross-tenure national feral pig management that continues to increase strategic regional coordination for sustained feral pig management; raise community awareness and capacity to apply humane, best practice management; and increase the adoption of best practice methods and systems.
In August 2025, new grant awarded to Australian Pork Limited (APL) to continue delivery of the National Feral Pig Management Coordination Program.
National Wild Dog Management Coordination Program
Delivered by: Australian Wool Innovation Ltd
This grant provides co-investment to support effective delivery of the National Wild Dog Action Plan 2020 -2030 that provides the national strategy to coordinate management of wild dogs and seeks to increase community awareness, understanding, capacity and adoption of humane, best practice wild dog management to reduce impacts on agricultural productivity and the environment.
This includes strengthening stakeholder involvement and facilitating cross-tenure management; raising community awareness and capacity to apply humane, best practice management; and increase the adoption of best practice methods and systems.
National Feral Deer Management Coordination Program
Delivered by: Department of Primary Industries and Regions South Australia
This grant will support delivery of the National Feral Deer Action Plan 2022-2027 to actively suppress feral deer populations to reduce their impact on Australia’s agricultural productivity, environment, significant places of high conservation value, and communities.
This includes coordinating cross-tenure feral deer management across all states with particular emphasis on strategies to contain large populations, and control of small populations before they spread to protect high value assets.
In August 2025, new grant awarded to Primary Industries and Regions South Australia (PIRSA) to continue delivery of the National Feral Deer Management Coordination Program.
National Feral Cat and Fox Management Coordination Program
Delivered by: Centre for Invasive Species Solutions
This grant supports active promotion of best management tools to control feral cats and foxes, to reduce impact on agricultural production and environment assets, including a focus on the 20 places identified in the Threatened Species Action Plan 2022-2032.
This includes coordination of cross-tenure feral cat and fox management across all states and territories, and promotion of best practice tools for effective and humane control of feral cats and foxes.
In August 2025, new grant awarded to the Centre for Invasive Species Solutions (CISS) to continue delivery of the National Feral Cat and Fox Management Coordination Program.

National Rabbit Management Coordination Program
Delivered by: Centre for Invasive Species Solutions
Provides funding to co-invest in a 2-year national rabbit management coordinator program to support best practice rabbit management.
The National rabbit management coordinator, Heidi Kleinert, was appointed in late 2024. Initial priorities include a communication and engagement plan to increase adoption of best practice integrated rabbit management; update of the pestSmart rabbit toolkit and glovebox guide; and coordination of a review of national rabbit standard operating procedures.
Further information on the National Coordinators can be found at Priority pest animals.
National Weed Biocontrol Pipeline Strategy – initial phase
Delivered by: Centre for Invasive Species Solutions, partnering with CSIRO, Queensland Department of Agriculture and Fisheries, Agriculture Victoria, New South Wales Department of Primary Industries, Atlas of Living Australia and Wild Matters.
This contract will address the gap in national priority setting for weed biocontrol. A national weed biocontrol RD&E Alliance has been established. A priority setting framework has been developed. The Framework will be applied to produce a priority list of candidates for weed biocontrol. An estimate of costs to implement 5-year RD&E plans will be provided. All deliverables will be considered by the Environment and Invasives Committee.
Further information on the Strategy can be found at Weeds Australia.
Reducing residual agricultural losses caused by feral pigs
Delivered by: Australian Pork Limited
There remains significant opportunity to reduce residual agricultural losses (and impact on natural assets) caused by feral pigs, increasing agricultural productivity and profitability which has flow on benefits to regional communities. The report will provide a useful input for governments, industry and community organisations working to reduce the impacts of feral pigs on agricultural production in Australia, including opportunities to extend engagement and networks, priority capacity building activities by industry and location and what other support/tools are needed to sustain long-term effective management.
