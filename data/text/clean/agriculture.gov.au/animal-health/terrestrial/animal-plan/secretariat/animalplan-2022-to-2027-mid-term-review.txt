Overview 
Animalplan 2022 to 2027 (Animalplan) is Australia’s first national action plan for production 
animal health. It aims to strengthen animal health practices and position Australian industries 
to meet emerging trends and requirements in a globally competitive market.   
This report summarises the key considerations of the Animalplan mid-term review workshop 
held on Wednesday 18th June 2025. These are based on the views of workshop participants – 
principally terrestrial production animal health industries, governments, and non-government 
organisations. 
Participants contributed actively (both online and in person) and significant effort was made to 
distil those inputs and align them with Animalplan’s strategic components. 
Mid-term review objectives 
The workshop aimed to support successful implementation of Animalplan by: 
1. Strengthening engagement between industry, government, and other organisations. 
2. Broadening stakeholder understanding of Animalplan, its purpose and opportunities. 
3. Highlighting and reflecting on the key achievements to date. 
4. Identifying if Animalplan is adequately achieving its objectives and addressing identified 
challenges. 
5. Exploring any need to amend objectives and activities that may not be achieving 
Animalplan’s intended outcomes. 
6. Identifying any gaps to ensure these are addressed where possible, including assigning 
responsibility to any new activities or actions arising from the workshop discussions. 
The review process  
The workshop structure included an introduction from the First Assistant Secretary of 
Biosecurity Animal Division and National Animal Disease Preparedness Coordinator, 
presentations from the Animalplan Steering Committee Co-Chairs and Secretariat, and 
presentations on AQUAPLAN and the Australian Agricultural Sustainability Framework (AASF) 
which both shared lessons learnt that participants could consider in relation to Animalplan. 
Participant feedback was collected through numerous individual and table activities, with 
ongoing room discussions, and question and answer sessions. 
The agenda covered the following topics: 
1. Workshop purpose and objectives including an introduction to Animalplan 2022 to 2027 
2. Lessons learnt from AQUAPLAN 
3. Identifying areas of underinvestment and clarifying Animalplan’s role  
4. Bringing together Australian industry for a common purpose - presentation on the AASF 
5. Communications and engagement 
6. Animalplan governance  
7. Future planning  
8. Outcomes, recommendations and action items

Review outcomes and recommendations 
This section summarises the key participant feedback received on Animalplan, its 
implementation and governance processes, and its individual objectives.  
1. General considerations 
a. Overwhelmingly workshop participants agreed that Animalplan was on track to achieve 
its goals, and the workshop was effective in improving understanding of Animalplan and 
how stakeholders can engage with the process. Stakeholders identified opportunities 
for improving the visibility of the projects that sit under Animalplan, and for enhanced 
stakeholder engagement in the remainder of this iteration. Suggested improvements to 
the content of Animalplan’s objectives will be carried over for consideration in the end-
of-term review and Animalplan’s next iteration. 
b. There was uncertainty about Animalplan’s relationship to other government strategies, 
and the outcomes it is meant to deliver. This suggests a need for more targeted 
guidance on implementation, and greater visibility of what success looks like. Ensuring 
stakeholders understand roles, overlaps, and points of influence, will enhance 
coordination and participation. 
c. The work of many organisations in animal health, surveillance, biosecurity, and welfare 
either pre-dates or operates independently of Animalplan. However, the work still aligns 
closely with Animalplan's objectives, indicating that Animalplan is useful as a reference 
point, alignment mechanism and amplifier, rather than the central driver of activity. 
Making the existing activities more visible and clearly linked to Animalplan would 
highlight its value, strengthen its coordinating role, share learnings, and encourage 
broader involvement and harmonised approaches to complex, cross-sector biosecurity 
issues and shared challenges. 
d. Improved communications would increase awareness and participation, particularly 
among stakeholders who may see certain topics as someone else’s responsibility, who 
may lack visibility of national efforts, and with less-engaged sectors including those 
unsure of how their activities contribute to and align with Animalplan’s goals. 
e. Clarifying Animalplan’s value and benefits would help smaller, niche and resource-
constrained organisations engage in ways that align with their mandates and capacity.

3. Animalplan implementation 
Participant feedback identified a broad range of activities for potential action or improvement. 
a. Enhance visibility within systems: integrate Animalplan updates and reporting into 
existing sector channels, forums and working groups e.g. AHC agendas, peak body 
meetings. 
b. Continue investment in ongoing dialogue platforms that enable cross-sector and cross-
jurisdictional collaboration to strengthen system resilience and continuous 
improvement. 
c. Facilitate cross-agency coordination on high-impact, cross-cutting issues such as 
vaccine access and regulatory readiness. 
d. Invest in enabling functions, including sector forums, collaboration platforms, and 
small-organisation capacity building.

e. Legitimacy was seen to depend not on ownership of activities, but on responsiveness to 
gaps, challenges, and changing sector needs. Particularly in the wake of emergency 
animal disease (EAD) events or preparedness exercises e.g. for lumpy skin disease 
(LSD), Animalplan’s relevance was seen to increase when it helped capture learnings, 
identify weak points, and support system upgrades across multiple objectives. 
f. Implement clear, efficient priority-setting processes respecting stakeholder time 
constraints, and with consistent prioritisation criteria grounded in value and impact. 
g. Engage expert advisory bodies to guide risk-based prioritisation. 
h. Use data-driven frameworks and international experience to inform investments. 
Explore technology solutions to support decision-making and resource management. 
j. 
Encourage innovation through both structured and informal idea generation. 
k. Foster discussions that link sector-specific concerns to overarching system issues.

4. Animalplan Steering Committee 
Participants sought an increased understanding of the Steering Committee’s role and 
processes. 
a. Clarify the Steering Committee’s role, contact pathway and governance processes, 
including insight into the Committee’s schedule, decision-making processes, and 
reporting requirements.  
b. Define roles and governance especially between the Animalplan Steering Committee 
and Secretariat. 
c. Simplify governance to reduce administrative load and focus on procedures, actionable 
priorities, partnerships and outcomes, while ensuring accountability and emphasising 
proactive engagement and facilitation.

6. Actions to improve Animalplan’s Objectives 
Participants provided feedback on projects and activities being undertaken in alignment with 
Animalplan’s objectives. The Steering Committee encourages stakeholders to consider where 
actions could be taken. 
6.1 Objective 1 – Activities to improve preparedness and ability to respond to 
emergency animal diseases 
To further strengthen Objective 1 delivery, future efforts may benefit from: 
a. Sustaining investment in capability, especially in: 
Training and preparedness simulations. 
ii. 
Laboratory capacity and diagnostic innovation. 
iii. 
On-farm biosecurity education and uptake. 
b. Ensuring national consistency, while respecting jurisdictional nuances, especially in 
remote and cross-border areas. 
6.2 Objective 2 – Improve Australia’s surveillance and diagnostic capacity for 
animal pests and diseases 
To strengthen the delivery and visibility of Objective 2, stakeholders could focus on: 
a. Promoting adoption of innovation: continuing to support diagnostic and data 
innovations (e.g. mobile laboratories, point-of-care tools, and data system 
improvements) and connecting these into national infrastructure and policy. 
b. Elevating and reinforcing the strategic role of surveillance beyond compliance, 
including in supporting trade, public health, early detection, and One Health priorities.

Page 11 of 14 
c. Encouraging and clarifying cross-jurisdictional coordination: multiple actors contribute 
to surveillance systems, and shared data strategies may enhance value and avoid 
duplication. 
6.3 Objective 3 – Improve the adoption and implementation of biosecurity 
practices throughout the terrestrial animal industry supply chain 
To further advance and unify supply chain biosecurity under Animalplan, the following areas 
may warrant attention: 
a. Continuing to foster tailored, sector-specific initiatives: successful models (like One 
Biosecurity or pig-focused work) could be scaled or adapted for other sectors with 
support from Animalplan frameworks. 
b. Bridging communication gaps along the supply chain: targeted efforts to reach harder-
to-engage actors (e.g. smallholders, independent transport operators) could ensure 
broader uptake of biosecurity practices. 
c. Using national platforms to better align daily operations of jurisdictions and industries 
under a stronger, more visible national framework. Regular collaboration with cross-
sector bodies can identify common gaps, and improve consistency, value, and 
innovation in supply chain biosecurity. 
d. While some responses showed strong information-sharing with stakeholders, more 
systematic mechanisms to evaluate and report on how biosecurity practices are 
adopted may enhance accountability and continuous improvement.  
6.4 Objective 4 – Manage the risk of antimicrobial resistance (AMR) 
To enhance national coherence and broaden engagement under Objective 4, stakeholders may 
consider: 
a. Expanding diagnostics investment and collaboration: there is a clear recognition that 
diagnostics plays a foundational role in AMR surveillance and stewardship, but 
agreements on national standards and investment in capacity is uneven. Coordinated 
investment could yield cross-sectoral benefits. 
b. Sector-specific framing and education: AMR priorities may not resonate equally across 
all industries. Animalplan could support targeted communication or capacity-building 
tailored to sectors (e.g. poultry, aquaculture, extensive livestock) to encourage greater 
participation. 
c. Leveraging cross-government forums: the alignment between Animalplan and broader 
AMR strategies (e.g. Australia’s National AMR Strategy, One Health frameworks) could 
be strengthened to minimise duplication and encourage synergy. 
d. Broadening the implementation base: responses suggest the work is being led by a few 
key organisations. A model distributing responsibility (supported by clearer 
expectations and coordination) may help embed AMR awareness and action more 
widely.

6.5 Objective 5 – Improve Animal Welfare Outcomes Relevant to Emergency 
Scenarios 
To better achieve national impact under Objective 5, stakeholders could consider the following: 
a. Clarifying the objective’s scope beyond emergency animal disease scenarios to 
explicitly include natural disasters, infrastructure failures and climate related events. 
While welfare during emergencies is well-acknowledged, broader improvements in 
animal welfare practices across industries may require clearer framing and articulation, 
especially in communications and planning guidance. 
Note: Changes to the content of Animalplan 2022 to 2027 will be carried over for 
consideration in the end-of-term review and next iteration. 
b. Addressing infrastructure & regulatory bottlenecks, including effluent management, 
vaccine access and fair support for small producers. 
c. Supporting ethical decision-making frameworks to help navigate tensions between 
individual, farm and population-level welfare outcomes. 
d. Proactively managing political and social sensitivities - trusted processes, strong 
leadership and science-based communication. 
e. Investing in public-facing communications strategies that bridge expectations gap to 
inform dialogue about animal welfare in real-world emergency contexts. 
f. Resourcing national leadership & advocacy roles that give producers a voice and ensure 
policies are credible, grounded and co-designed with industry and the community 
where relevant. 
g. Improving visibility of sector-specific gaps and opportunities to surface under-
addressed issues and invite new partnerships or solutions. 
h. Engaging and equipping less-involved stakeholders: some participants may support 
animal welfare implicitly (e.g. through general animal health or biosecurity programs) 
but don’t see their contribution reflected in this objective. Clearer definitions and 
examples of what “counts” as supporting this objective could increase alignment and 
participation. 
Leveraging expertise more effectively: organisations contributing through technical 
advice or research provide high-value input. Animalplan could better acknowledge and 
integrate these roles within its national reporting and coordination structures. 
6.6 Objective 6 – Implement industry sustainability frameworks and plans 
To strengthen the role and impact of Animalplan under Objective 6, stakeholders may consider: 
a. Respecting and reinforcing industry leadership: sustainability is best developed and 
owned by sectors themselves. Animalplan should continue to act as an enabler, not a 
director, of sustainability initiatives, supporting knowledge-sharing and alignment 
without duplicating existing frameworks.

Page 13 of 14 
b. Supporting sectors at varying levels of maturity: not all industries have the same 
resources or progress in developing sustainability plans. Stakeholders can work with 
the Animalplan Secretariat and Steering Committee to identify gaps, offer templates or 
guidance, and provide a platform for emerging industries to learn from more advanced 
sectors. 
c. Building cross-objective links: sustainability intersects with biosecurity, market access, 
welfare, and system integrity. Making these cross-cutting benefits more explicit may 
help engage organisations who don’t traditionally see themselves as working in 
sustainability, but who influence it through core operations. 
6.7 Objective 7 – Improve the integrity of animal health systems 
To enhance implementation and visibility of Objective 7, stakeholders may consider: 
a. Expanding traceability coverage to sectors where current gaps pose risks to EAD 
response, trade integrity or animal welfare outcomes. 
b. Improving strategic alignment of traceability investment, ensuring resources are 
directed based on risk, sector readiness and public good outcomes, not just legacy 
systems or ease of delivery. 
c. Strengthening activity 7.2 by enabling data interoperability across supply chains, 
ensuring all participants can access and benefit from traceability data. 
d. Elevating and clarifying activity 7.3 by embedding feedback mechanisms that deliver 
actionable insights to producers and drive continuous improvement across animal 
health systems. 
e. Integrating traceability with regulatory or diagnostic systems, creating a more holistic 
approach to health intelligence and supply chain transparency. 
f. Reducing system fragmentation by improving interagency coordination, supporting 
smaller or emerging sectors, and ensure equitable regulatory environments across 
species. 
g. Continuing cross-jurisdictional collaboration, including through SAFEMEAT, NLIS 
governance and traceability taskforces, to drive consistent national outcomes while 
maintaining flexibility. Animalplan could help catalyse or coordinate innovation pilots. 
h. Improving understanding and relevance among niche or indirect stakeholders: clearer 
examples and targeted engagement may demonstrate how integrity in the animal health 
system relates to the work of those unsure of how they fit into Objective 7.

OBJECTIVES 1 TO 7 - ACTIONS: 
Many of the suggestions made during the workshop were directed towards improving the 
scope of the current objectives, or towards the outcomes of individual projects. Where this 
feedback can’t be acted on immediately, it will be incorporated into the end-of-cycle review 
process, to inform improvements that can be made in the next iteration of Animalplan.  
Where the feedback provided is relevant to Animalplan stakeholders more generally, the 
Steering Committee encourages everyone involved to incorporate potential improvements 
into their own work. This includes leveraging investments made by others, increasing the 
utility of projects for other sectors, and increasing the visibility of projects for the benefit of 
the entire Australian production animal sector.  
Conclusion 
Implementation of Animalplan activities will continue to be a shared responsibility between 
government, industry and non-government organisations. Collaboration and cooperation 
remain crucial to effectively implement the 21 activities that make up Animalplan. 
The Steering Committee thanks all participants for engaging in the mid-term review and 
encourages stakeholders to stay engaged in Animalplan 2022 to 2027. 
Information and updates on Animalplan and the projects under it are available at 
www.agriculture.gov.au/animal-plan. 
Enquiries can be directed to the Animalplan Secretariat at animalplan@aff.gov.au.
