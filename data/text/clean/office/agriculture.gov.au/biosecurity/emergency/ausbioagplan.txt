4. 	Introduction

4.1.	Purpose

4.2.	Scope

AUSBIOAGPLAN applies to:

domestic biosecurity crises – incidents relating to pests and diseases entering, emerging, establishing or spreading in Australian territory.

These incidents may include:

pests and diseases causing harm to animal health (terrestrial and aquatic)

pests and diseases causing harm to plant health

pests and diseases causing harm to Australia’s environment or economy.

4.3.	Out of scope

AUSBIOAGPLAN does not apply to the management of biosecurity incidents primarily impacting humans or public health (including those resulting from zoonoses). The Department of Health, Disability and Ageing is the responsible for the management of biosecurity incidents primarily impacting humans or public health.

4.4.	Principles

Crises will be managed in accordance with relevant legislative and ministerial authority and in accordance with existing coordination arrangements, including the relevant national biosecurity emergency response agreements (where applicable). See Appendix A and Appendix B.

Engagement with relevant state and territory agriculture agencies and peak industry bodies will occur at all levels of operation, including through the National Management Group (NMG) and Consultative Committee established under the relevant national biosecurity emergency response agreements.

Unity of effort is supported through centralised strategic planning and coordination. When the scale or complexity of an event requires national leadership across jurisdictions or agencies, the level of coordination is scalable and flexible relative to the situation.

Impacts and consequences will be assessed and managed throughout all phases to inform strategic priorities, support affected communities, and guide recovery planning.

State and territory governments maintain legislation and related arrangements to prevent, prepare for, respond to, and recover from hazards within their jurisdiction.

4.5.	Consideration of diverse groups

Under AUSBIOAGPLAN, decisions and crisis communication made through preparedness, response, and early recovery phases will consider the needs of people who may be disproportionately at risk of harm and ensure they are included in preparedness planning and considered during response and recovery activities. This may include:

the recognition of the unique, environmental and cultural responsibilities of First Nations people and communities

rural and/or regional communities experiencing high levels of stress, burden or vulnerability

people who have limited English, including refugee and migrant populations, including workers under labour mobility schemes

people experiencing or at risk of gender-based violence

culturally and linguistically diverse communities.

5.	Roles and responsibilities

5.1. 	Lead Minister

Under the AGCMF, the Lead Minister for AUSBIOAGPLAN is the Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry.

The Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry is responsible for:

ensuring there are systems and procedures available so that they are readily contactable

determining if a disease or pest is posing a severe and immediate threat, or is causing harm, on a nationally significant scale to animal or plant health, the environment or economic activities related to animals, plants or the environment and advise the Governor-General to declare a biosecurity emergency if the declaration is necessary to prevent or control the establishment or spread of the disease or pest in Australian territory or part of Australian territory.

Should a crisis require Tier 4 coordination as defined by the AGCMF, the Prime Minister will become the Lead Minister. The Prime Minister may delegate some, or all responsibilities, to another minister.

DAFF is responsible for:

maintaining a surge cohort of appropriately trained staff with appropriate security clearances to coordinate and respond to a domestic biosecurity or agricultural crisis

coordinating across Australian, state and territory governments to respond to domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises

leading consequence management activities to predict, identify, manage and minimise impacts of domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises

using the national biosecurity emergency response agreements to support decision-making and facilitate unity of effort, including through the National Management Group and Consultative Committees, or other sector-specific committees

supporting the Lead Coordinating Senior Official to make decisions and recommendations on the level of coordination and response required

undertaking, or arranging for others to undertake, on-ground biosecurity control operations in Commonwealth places (such as Australian external territories, first points of entry, Indigenous Protected Areas and areas managed by Commonwealth agencies such as the land and waters used by the Department of Defence), in consultation with state and territory agencies and relevant First Nation communities

identifying capability and resource needs and leveraging surge arrangements to fulfil requirements

deploying or receiving liaison officers as required to support a coordinated response

maintaining continuity of DAFF critical business functions

maintaining records of decisions and actions relevant to domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises

transferring any continuing functions and responsibilities to relevant officials and agencies (including state and territory leads) and recovery coordination mechanisms, as crisis coordination concludes

5.3. 	Lead Coordinating Senior Official

The Lead Coordinating Senior Official is the DAFF Deputy Secretary responsible for Biosecurity and is responsible for:

advising, briefing and supporting the Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry to execute their responsibilities

requesting NEMA to convene and co-chair the NCM with the Deputy Coordinator General of National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA), if required

engaging with counterparts in state, territory or foreign governments and maintaining oversight of any requests or offers of assistance

co-opting people and resources internally as appropriate to resolve the crisis

ensuring records of decisions and actions are maintained

ensuring the transfer of any continuing functions and responsibilities to relevant officials and agencies (including state and territory leads) and recovery coordination mechanisms as crisis coordination concludes

determining when the Active phase ends and overseeing deactivation arrangements, including evaluations, debriefs and after-action reviews.

Should a crisis require Tier 4 coordination as defined by the AGCMF, the Lead Coordinating Senior Official role will transfer to Deputy Coordinator General, Emergency Management and Response, NEMA.

5.4.	Australian Chief Veterinary Officer, Australian Chief Plant Protection Officer, Australian Chief Environmental Biosecurity Officer

The relevant Chief Officer is responsible for:

receiving notifications of actual or suspected domestic biosecurity incidents

notifying the Lead Coordinating Senior Official of actual or suspected domestic biosecurity incidents, if required

coordinating with Australian, state and territory government agency counterparts as appropriate, including the Director of Human Biosecurity regarding potential public health ramifications

chairing the relevant CC and leading the technical response to domestic biosecurity crises

advising the NMG on response strategies

collaborating with international counterparts, including notifications to World Organisation for Animal Health or the International Plant Protection Convention secretariat.

6.	Operational activities

6.1.	Authority

The DAFF Secretary (or a person nominated by the DAFF Secretary) has the authority to initiate phase changes under AUSBIOAGPLAN.

A phase change from STANDBY to ALERT or ACTIVE will be authorised if an event to which AUSBIOAGPLAN applies is imminent or occurring.

6.2.	Phases and actions

AUSBIOAGPLAN consists of 3 phases:

STANDBY

ALERT

ACTIVE.

STANDBY

STANDBY is the default phase of AUSBIOAGPLAN.

The following activities are undertaken in STANDBY:

Developing, training, exercising and evaluate the capabilities required to use AUSBIOAGPLAN.

Undertaking contingency planning for potential responses, including maintaining business continuity plans.

Reviewing and maintain AUSBIOAGPLAN.

AUSBIOAGPLAN reverts to STANDBY when ACTIVE finishes or when the ALERT phase expires and does not proceed to ACTIVE.

ALERT

ALERT phase may be authorised when there is a reasonably held suspicion that an event to which AUSBIOAGPLAN applies is likely to occur.

The following activities are undertaken in ALERT:

Advising key stakeholders that AUSBIOAGPLAN has moved to ALERT.

Establishing and maintaining shared situational awareness.

Undertaking Crisis Appreciation and Strategic Planning (CASP) to identify potential impacts and consequences.

Considering thresholds and triggers that may require the establishment of the NCM.

Bringing DAFF capabilities to an enhanced level of readiness.

ALERT phase automatically reverts to STANDBY phase after 72 hours unless advised otherwise.

ACTIVE

ACTIVE phase may be authorised when an event to which AUSBIOAGPLAN applies is imminent or has occurred.

The following activities are undertaken in ACTIVE:

Advising key stakeholders that AUSBIOAGPLAN has moved to ACTIVE.

Activating DAFF crisis coordination arrangements, including the Response Coordination Group (RCG) and an Incident Management Team (IMT).

Activating the relevant national biosecurity emergency response agreements (if not already activated by an affected state or territory).

Establishing and maintaining shared situational awareness.

Using CASP methodology to identify impacts and consequences, agreed national strategic priorities and establish lines of effort.

Requesting NEMA to convene the NCM (in collaboration with DAFF) to provide situational awareness and coordinate response efforts, if required.

Coordinating with relevant Australian, state and territory government agencies, and industry peak bodies, including through the deployment or receipt of Liaison Officers.

Advising key stakeholders AUSBIOAGPLAN is moving to STANDBY.

Conducting debriefs or undertaking an after-action review.

ACTIVE phase ends when a decision is made by the Secretary on advice from the Lead Coordinating Senior Official.

6.4.	Tier-4 coordination

If a domestic biosecurity or agricultural crisis is determined to require Tier-4 crisis coordination under the AGCMF:

the Lead Minister role will transition to the Prime Minister who may wish to delegate some, or all, responsibilities to another minister

The transition does not displace portfolio specific responsibilities. Should a transition occur, DAFF will remain the Sector Lead Agency for agriculture, fisheries and forestry and will continue to lead consequence management activities to predict, identify, manage and minimise negative outcomes and impacts of domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises.

6.5.	Recovery

Consideration of recovery commences at the same time as response activities are implemented. Under most circumstances, responsibility for the management of recovery from crises that fall within the scope of AUSBIOAGPLAN will sit with state and territory government agencies.

This assistance may include:

the provision of financial assistance

national coordination functions.

Recovery efforts in Commonwealth places (such as Australian external territories, first points of entry, Indigenous Protected Areas and areas managed by Commonwealth agencies such as the land and waters used by the Department of Defence) will sit with the Commonwealth Place land manager, in consultation with state and territory agencies and relevant First Nation communities.

6.8	Crisis communication

During a biosecurity crisis DAFF will:

Develop and disseminate national talking points.

Coordinate crisis communication key messages and products with relevant states and territories.

Confirm key spokespeople and tempo of engagement.

Liaise with media and public information officers across government, and other stakeholders including media outlets as required.

Anticipate and resolve communication risks, including misinformation and disinformation.

Incorporate and prioritise coordinated and consistent key messages across government.

Adjust communications as the event, the media cycle and community needs evolve.

Support other areas of government that are organising media conferences.

National Biosecurity Communication and Engagement Network

The National Biosecurity Communication and Engagement Network (NBCEN) supports the national biosecurity response arrangements and advises the CC and NMG on best practice crisis communication. It provides a forum to support unity of effort and maintain national consistency of communication and engagement efforts for biosecurity events.

NBCEN comprises communication managers from Australian and state and territory agencies responsible for biosecurity, Animal Health Australia, and Plant Health Australia. NBCEN can also include communication managers from affected industries, other agencies or organisations critical to the response.

NBCEN will engage across governments, and with industry and stakeholders such as media outlets, to ensure relevant information is provided to the public in a timely manner.

6.9.	Resourcing and surge

The Lead Coordinating Senior Official may co-opt people and resources internally as appropriate to resolve the crisis. A prolonged biosecurity crisis or a crisis of significant scale or complexity could erode DAFF’s capacity to respond to a crisis event. When internal surge options are exhausted, DAFF may use surge workforce options across the Australian Public Service to augment its coordination arrangements. These options may include the surge reserve maintained by the Australian Public Service Commission (APSC), which mobilises staff in large numbers to respond to significant crises when required.

7.	Requests for assistance

7.1.	Domestic

The National Biosecurity Response Team (NBRT) is a group of trained and experienced personnel that may be deployed to assist a jurisdiction in the response to biosecurity incidents. NBRT members are personnel from government agencies with knowledge, experience and training in responding to biosecurity incidents. NBRT includes a functional cohort (personnel for working in a response team) and a more experienced mentor cohort (which can assist in setting up an initial response structure and provide guidance to less experienced response personnel). Deployment is managed using the Interstate Deployment Arrangements for Biosecurity Responses.

The Interstate Deployment Arrangements (IDA) for Biosecurity Responses are national arrangements to facilitate the sharing of non-financial resources between biosecurity agencies. DAFF may provide a national coordination function under these arrangements.

7.2.	International

The International Animal Health Emergency Reserve (IAHER) is a formal arrangement that provides participating countries access to additional human resources in the event of an emergency animal disease outbreak. Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, United Kingdom and United States of America are signatories.

8.	Financial arrangements

Agencies under AUSBIOAGPLAN are responsible for their own expenses consistent with their responsibilities. If assistance in the form of personnel or physical resources is provided across agencies or jurisdictions, financial considerations are managed according to the arrangements under which the assistance is provided.
