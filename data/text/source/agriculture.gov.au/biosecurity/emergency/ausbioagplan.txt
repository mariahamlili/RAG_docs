Definitions 
Australian Government 
Coordinating Agency 
The agency required to lead the coordination across the Australian 
Government for a significant crisis (or concurrent crises with 
converging consequences) caused by one or more identified hazards 
under the AGCMF. This agency also leads the consequence 
management activities within its agency functions and sector-specific 
responsibilities. 
Australian Government 
Crisis Communication 
Guidelines 
Provides guidance on coordinating Australian Government crisis 
communication, including development and dissemination of talking 
points, media holding lines and response strategies for media 
management and collaboration with state and territory operations 
centres. 
Australian Government 
Crisis Management 
Framework (AGCMF) 
The AGCMF is the Australian Government’s capstone policy framing 
Australia‘s national crisis management arrangements. 
Australian territory 
Australia, Christmas Island, Cocos (Keeling) Islands and their 
airspace and coastal seas and any external territory and its airspace 
and coastal seas to which the provisions of the Biosecurity Act 2015 
and its regulations extend. 
biosecurity 
Is the management of risks to Australia’s plant and animal health, the 
environment, or the economy, from pests and diseases entering, 
emerging, establishing or spreading in Australian territory or part of 
Australian territory. 
biosecurity emergency 
An incident where a disease or pest is posing a severe and 
immediate threat, or causing harm to Australia’s animal or plant 
health, the environment, or the economy, and requires a whole-of-
government response. 
biosecurity risk 
The likelihood of a disease or pest entering, emerging, establishing or 
spreading in Australian territory or a part of Australian territory and 
the potential for the disease or pest to harm animal or plant health, 
the environment, or the economy. 
consequence 
management 
Refers to a process undertaken to predict, identify, manage and 
minimise negative outcomes and impacts from crises. 
crisis 
An event that requires an immediate Australian Government 
response outside business-as-usual arrangements to manage 
potential or realised acute consequences and mitigate further harm.

Crisis Appreciation and 
Strategic Planning 
(CASP) 
The CASP is a structured, systematic methodology that uses 
strategic and critical thinking and conceptualises the big picture in 
crisis planning. It supports practitioners to manage challenges 
including concurrent events, consequences of uncertainty and 
complexity. It can be used by Australian, state and territory 
governments to conduct strategic planning and informs crisis 
decision-making at all levels. 
disease 
(a) the signs or symptoms of an illness or infection caused by a 
disease agent 
 (b) a collection of signs or symptoms that is clinically defined, for 
which the causal agent is unknown, or 
 (c) a disease agent that has the potential to cause, either directly or 
indirectly, an illness or infection. 
early recovery 
Temporary, near-term measures that support anticipated community 
needs, such as transitional shelter, services and supplies. During 
early recovery, the restoration of critical infrastructure would also be 
underway. This may occur alongside operational response and relief 
efforts.

An Australian Government agency that administers relevant 
programs, provides specialist technical, scientific, intelligence or 
information capabilities, service delivery or conducts any other 
enabling activities to support consequence management activities. 
environmental 
biosecurity

ecosystems and their constituent parts and natural physical 
resources, from pests and diseases entering, emerging, establishing 
or spreading in Australian territory or part of Australian territory.

3. 
Authorising environment  
3.1. Authority 
The Australian Government Biosecurity and Agricultural Response Plan (AUSBIOAGPLAN) derives its 
authority from the Australian Government Crisis Management Framework (AGCMF). The AGCMF outlines 
the roles and responsibilities of the Australian Government in preparing for, responding to and recovering 
from crises. It is the Australian Government’s capstone policy framing Australia’s national crisis management 
arrangements. 
AUSBIOAGPLAN is maintained by the Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry (DAFF). It provides 
a mechanism for Australian Government coordination of domestic biosecurity and agricultural crises. 
The minister responsible for this plan is the Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry. 
AUSBIOAGPLAN is approved by the Secretary of the Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry. 
3.2. Review and amendments 
This Plan is subject to a major review at least once every 3 years or when relevant updates are made to the 
AGCMF. Recommendations for amendments or suggestions for improvement may be forwarded to: 
Crisis and Emergency Management Branch 
Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry 
Email: CEMB@aff.gov.au.

4.  Introduction 
4.1. Purpose 
AUSBIOAGPLAN outlines the mechanisms and coordination arrangements for an Australian Government 
response to domestic biosecurity crises. The plan can also apply to events impacting the agricultural sector 
where no other Australian Government plan applies under the Australian Government Crisis Management 
Framework (AGCMF). 
4.2. Scope 
AUSBIOAGPLAN applies to: 
a) domestic biosecurity crises – incidents relating to pests and diseases entering, emerging, establishing 
or spreading in Australian territory. 
These incidents may include: 
• 
pests and diseases causing harm to animal health (terrestrial and aquatic) 
• 
pests and diseases causing harm to plant health 
• 
pests and diseases causing harm to Australia’s environment or economy. 
b) agriculture crises – incidents impacting the agricultural sector (agriculture, fisheries and forestry) where 
DAFF will lead Australian Government coordination. 
4.3. Out of scope 
AUSBIOAGPLAN does not apply to the management of biosecurity incidents primarily impacting humans or 
public health (including those resulting from zoonoses). The Department of Health, Disability and Ageing is 
the responsible for the management of biosecurity incidents primarily impacting humans or public health. 
4.4. Principles 
1. Crises will be managed in accordance with relevant legislative and ministerial authority and in accordance 
with existing coordination arrangements, including the relevant national biosecurity emergency response 
agreements (where applicable). See Appendix A and Appendix B. 
2. Engagement with Australian Government agencies will occur at all levels of operation. Agencies will 
coordinate internally and with their state or territory counterparts (where appropriate) in line with their 
sector-specific arrangements and mechanisms. 
3. Engagement with relevant state and territory agriculture agencies and peak industry bodies will occur at 
all levels of operation, including through the National Management Group (NMG) and Consultative 
Committee established under the relevant national biosecurity emergency response agreements. 
4. Unity of effort is supported through centralised strategic planning and coordination. When the scale or 
complexity of an event requires national leadership across jurisdictions or agencies, the level of 
coordination is scalable and flexible relative to the situation. 
5. Impacts and consequences will be assessed and managed throughout all phases to inform strategic 
priorities, support affected communities, and guide recovery planning. 
6. State and territory governments maintain legislation and related arrangements to prevent, prepare for, 
respond to, and recover from hazards within their jurisdiction.

4.5. Consideration of diverse groups 
Under AUSBIOAGPLAN, decisions and crisis communication made through preparedness, response, and 
early recovery phases will consider the needs of people who may be disproportionately at risk of harm and 
ensure they are included in preparedness planning and considered during response and recovery activities. 
This may include: 
1. the recognition of the unique, environmental and cultural responsibilities of First Nations people and 
communities 
2. rural and/or regional communities experiencing high levels of stress, burden or vulnerability 
3. people who have limited English, including refugee and migrant populations, including workers under 
labour mobility schemes 
4. people experiencing or at risk of gender-based violence 
5. culturally and linguistically diverse communities. 
5. 
Roles and responsibilities 
AUSBIOAGPLAN establishes clear responsibilities to ensure Australian Government coordination and 
response activities are aligned and responsive to impacts and consequences of domestic biosecurity and 
agricultural crises on the economy, environment or community. 
The responsibilities of the Lead Minister, Australian Government Coordinating Agency, Lead Coordinating 
Senior Official, Sector Lead Agencies and Enabling Agencies under AUSBIOAGPLAN are outlined in 
section 5.1. 
5.1.  Lead Minister 
Under the AGCMF, the Lead Minister for AUSBIOAGPLAN is the Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and 
Forestry.  
The Minister for Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry is responsible for: 
1. providing advice to the Prime Minister and the National Security Committee of Cabinet (or other 
Committees of Cabinet) on whole of Australian Government crisis priorities and consequence 
management objectives for domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises 
2. overseeing a coordinated Australian Government response to domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises 
by collaborating with other Australian Government and jurisdictional counterparts 
3. exercising executive responsibilities and decision-making in consultation with Australian Government 
ministers with relevant interests 
4. ensuring there are systems and procedures available so that they are readily contactable 
5. acting as the key Australian Government spokesperson in alignment with a whole of Australian 
Government crisis communications strategy 
6. determining if a disease or pest is posing a severe and immediate threat, or is causing harm, on a 
nationally significant scale to animal or plant health, the environment or economic activities related to 
animals, plants or the environment and advise the Governor-General to declare a biosecurity emergency 
if the declaration is necessary to prevent or control the establishment or spread of the disease or pest in 
Australian territory or part of Australian territory. 
Should a crisis require Tier 4 coordination as defined by the AGCMF, the Prime Minister will become the 
Lead Minister. The Prime Minister may delegate some, or all responsibilities, to another minister.

5.2.  Australian Government Coordinating Agency 
Under the AGCMF, DAFF is the Australian Government Coordinating Agency for domestic biosecurity crises. 
DAFF may also become the Australian Government Coordinating Agency for other crises impacting the 
agricultural sector.  
DAFF is responsible for: 
1. maintaining a surge cohort of appropriately trained staff with appropriate security clearances to coordinate 
and respond to a domestic biosecurity or agricultural crisis 
2. coordinating across Australian, state and territory governments to respond to domestic biosecurity or 
agricultural crises 
3. leading consequence management activities to predict, identify, manage and minimise impacts of 
domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises 
4. monitoring events that may impact DAFF equities and notifying the National Situation Room (NSR) of 
significant events that may occur or are occurring to ensure situational awareness across Australian 
Government 
5. providing advice on the requirement for, or requesting, the establishment of Australian Government 
decision-making and coordination mechanisms such as the National Coordination Mechanism (NCM) 
6. using the national biosecurity emergency response agreements to support decision-making and facilitate 
unity of effort, including through the National Management Group and Consultative Committees, or other 
sector-specific committees 
7. supporting the Lead Coordinating Senior Official to make decisions and recommendations on the level of 
coordination and response required 
8. undertaking, or arranging for others to undertake, on-ground biosecurity control operations in 
Commonwealth places (such as Australian external territories, first points of entry, Indigenous Protected 
Areas and areas managed by Commonwealth agencies such as the land and waters used by the 
Department of Defence), in consultation with state and territory agencies and relevant First Nation 
communities 
9. identifying capability and resource needs and leveraging surge arrangements to fulfil requirements 
10. developing and coordinating an Australian Government crisis communications strategy and supporting 
products (such as whole of government talking points and briefing material) 
11. deploying or receiving liaison officers as required to support a coordinated response 
12. maintaining continuity of DAFF critical business functions 
13. maintaining records of decisions and actions relevant to domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises 
14. transferring any continuing functions and responsibilities to relevant officials and agencies (including state 
and territory leads) and recovery coordination mechanisms, as crisis coordination concludes 
15. conducting exercises and evaluations to identify lessons and contribute to continuous improvement of 
Australian Government crisis management arrangements. 
If the role of Australian Government Coordinating Agency is transferred from DAFF to another agency, DAFF 
still leads consequence management activities to predict, identify, manage and minimise negative outcomes 
and impacts of domestic biosecurity or agricultural crises.

10. co-opting people and resources internally as appropriate to resolve the crisis

6. 
Operational activities 
6.1. Authority 
The DAFF Secretary (or a person nominated by the DAFF Secretary) has the authority to initiate phase 
changes under AUSBIOAGPLAN. 
A phase change from STANDBY to ALERT or ACTIVE will be authorised if an event to which 
AUSBIOAGPLAN applies is imminent or occurring. 
DAFF will advise relevant Australian Government agencies and jurisdictions of phase changes to 
AUSBIOAGPLAN. 
6.2. Phases and actions 
AUSBIOAGPLAN consists of 3 phases: 
• STANDBY 
• ALERT 
• ACTIVE. 
STANDBY 
STANDBY is the default phase of AUSBIOAGPLAN. 
The following activities are undertaken in STANDBY: 
1. Monitoring potential risks and events that may impact Australian Government equities and the agricultural 
sector. 
2. Developing, training, exercising and evaluate the capabilities required to use AUSBIOAGPLAN. 
3. Undertaking contingency planning for potential responses, including maintaining business continuity 
plans. 
4. Reviewing and maintain AUSBIOAGPLAN. 
AUSBIOAGPLAN reverts to STANDBY when ACTIVE finishes or when the ALERT phase expires and does 
not proceed to ACTIVE.

Page 16 of 36 
 
OFFICIAL
ALERT 
ALERT phase may be authorised when there is a reasonably held suspicion that an event to which 
AUSBIOAGPLAN applies is likely to occur. 
The following activities are undertaken in ALERT: 
1. Advising key stakeholders that AUSBIOAGPLAN has moved to ALERT. 
2. Establishing and maintaining shared situational awareness. 
3. Undertaking Crisis Appreciation and Strategic Planning (CASP) to identify potential impacts and 
consequences. 
4. Considering thresholds and triggers that may require the establishment of the NCM. 
5. Bringing DAFF capabilities to an enhanced level of readiness. 
ALERT phase automatically reverts to STANDBY phase after 72 hours unless advised otherwise. 
ACTIVE 
ACTIVE phase may be authorised when an event to which AUSBIOAGPLAN applies is imminent or has 
occurred. 
The following activities are undertaken in ACTIVE: 
1. Advising key stakeholders that AUSBIOAGPLAN has moved to ACTIVE. 
2. Activating DAFF crisis coordination arrangements, including the Response Coordination Group (RCG) 
and an Incident Management Team (IMT). 
3. Activating the relevant national biosecurity emergency response agreements (if not already activated by 
an affected state or territory). 
4. Establishing and maintaining shared situational awareness. 
5. Using CASP methodology to identify impacts and consequences, agreed national strategic priorities and 
establish lines of effort. 
6. Requesting NEMA to convene the NCM (in collaboration with DAFF) to provide situational awareness and 
coordinate response efforts, if required. 
7. Developing and coordinating a crisis communication strategy, including the dissemination of whole-of-
Australian Government talking points. 
8. Coordinating with relevant Australian, state and territory government agencies, and industry peak bodies, 
including through the deployment or receipt of Liaison Officers. 
9. Once the crisis has been resolved or can be effectively managed without enhanced Australian 
Government coordination: 
10. Advising key stakeholders AUSBIOAGPLAN is moving to STANDBY. 
11. Conducting debriefs or undertaking an after-action review. 
ACTIVE phase ends when a decision is made by the Secretary on advice from the Lead Coordinating Senior 
Official.

1. assess the severity and complexity of a crisis and its impacts and consequences 
2. determine the appropriate level of crisis coordination and response mechanisms

4. manage responses to crisis and their consequences as they become clear 
5. adjust the coordination over time including the ability to change the Lead Minister and Australian

6.4. Tier-4 coordination 
Tier 4 coordination may be triggered by a domestic biosecurity or agricultural crisis when the complexity and 
severity of impacts and consequences requires the highest level of coordination across the Australian 
Government. 
If a domestic biosecurity or agricultural crisis is determined to require Tier-4 crisis coordination under the 
AGCMF: 
1. the Lead Minister role will transition to the Prime Minister who may wish to delegate some, or all, 
responsibilities to another minister 
2. the Deputy Secretary responsible for Biosecurity, as the Lead Coordinating Senior Official, will transition 
Australian Government Coordinating Agency responsibilities to NEMA, with the role of Lead Coordinating 
Senior Official transferring to Deputy Coordinator General, Emergency Management and Response, 
NEMA. 
The transition does not displace portfolio specific responsibilities. Should a transition occur, DAFF will remain 
the Sector Lead Agency for agriculture, fisheries and forestry and will continue to lead consequence 
management activities to predict, identify, manage and minimise negative outcomes and impacts of domestic 
biosecurity or agricultural crises. 
DAFF will support NEMA as the Australian Government Coordinating Agency following the transition. 
6.5. Recovery 
Consideration of recovery commences at the same time as response activities are implemented. Under most 
circumstances, responsibility for the management of recovery from crises that fall within the scope of 
AUSBIOAGPLAN will sit with state and territory government agencies. 
However, the Australian Government can provide direct recovery support to communities or to state and/or 
territory governments.

Page 18 of 36 
 
OFFICIAL
This assistance may include: 
1. the provision of Australian Government capabilities 
2. the provision of financial assistance 
3. national coordination functions. 
Recovery efforts in Commonwealth places (such as Australian external territories, first points of entry, 
Indigenous Protected Areas and areas managed by Commonwealth agencies such as the land and waters 
used by the Department of Defence) will sit with the Commonwealth Place land manager, in consultation 
with state and territory agencies and relevant First Nation communities. 
Where Australian Government recovery coordination is required, DAFF will work with NEMA through 
AGCMF arrangements, or directly with other Australian Government agencies and stakeholders to support 
this function. The need for Australian Government or nationally coordinated recovery efforts will be 
considered on a case-by-case basis. 
6.6. Deactivation 
Upon completion of all response actions and confirmation of no further requirement for Australian 
Government coordination, AUSBIOAGPLAN will revert to STANDBY. 
Following deactivation, DAFF will conduct a post-response evaluation and after-action review process to 
identify lessons. For Tier 3 or Tier 4 crisis coordination, an Australian Government evaluation process is 
required under the AGCMF. The outcomes of any review process will be distributed to relevant stakeholders, 
including Australian Government agencies, and will inform updates to AUSBIOAGPLAN and Australian 
Government crisis management arrangements. 
6.7. Coordination mechanisms and arrangements 
DAFF will engage with other Australian Government agencies, state and territory biosecurity and agriculture 
agencies and impacted industries primarily through the NCM, National Management Group, relevant 
Consultative Committee and State Coordination Centres. 
The NCM can be used for shared situational awareness, national coordination and synchronisation of effort 
under AUSBIOAGPLAN. It may bring together relevant Australian, state, territory and local government 
officials, industry, peak bodies, non-government organisations (NGOs) and eminent individuals for 
coordination, communication and collaboration. NCM participation can be restricted when required, as with 
the Australian Government NCM (NCM-AUSGOV). 
The structures in Figure 2 and described in this section outline how AUSBIOAGPLAN integrates with 
Australian Government coordination mechanisms, national biosecurity systems, and state and territory 
arrangements. 
Australian Government coordination mechanisms 
National Coordination Mechanism (NCM) 
The NCM is the peak senior officials’ crisis coordination mechanism providing a national picture of crisis to 
governments and key stakeholders. The NCM provides the convening mechanism to bring together 
Australian Government, state and territory government and nongovernment representatives immediately 
before, during and after a crisis. 
When AUSBIOAGPLAN is in ALERT and or ACTIVE phase the NCM may be used for coordination of near 
term preparedness activities, maintain situational awareness, ensure strategic alignment, agree to national 
priorities and objectives and synchronise Australian Government and national activities.

National biosecurity emergency response agreements 
Australia’s biosecurity system includes sector specific national biosecurity emergency response agreements 
(cost sharing deeds). The following coordination mechanisms enable collaborative decision-making under 
the cost sharing deeds. 
National Management Group (NMG) 
The NMG is the decision-making body for national biosecurity emergency responses. The NMG approves 
national pest or disease emergency response plans and agrees to the proportional sharing of costs between 
affected parties. It comprises Australian Government, states and territories and relevant industry parties. 
DAFF chairs, participates in and provides secretariat support to the group. 
Consultative Committee (CC) 
The CC is the technical body that agrees to the technical response to national biosecurity emergencies and 
advises the NMG on response strategies. It comprises Australian Government, state and territories and 
affected industry parties. DAFF chairs, participates in, and provides secretariat support to consultative 
committees. 
DAFF coordination mechanisms 
Response Coordination Group (RCG) 
The RCG provides an agency-wide strategic coordination function to facilitate leadership and oversight of 
DAFF’s response efforts. The RCG supports shared situational awareness across the department. The RCG 
is led by a Response Coordinator and comprises senior executive service (SES) representation. The 
Response Coordinator may seek to co-opt the support of representatives of other Australian Government 
agencies to support enhanced coordination where AGCMF mechanisms have not been activated. 
Incident Management Team (IMT) 
The IMT manages DAFF’s operational response and provides a centralised command and control capability 
within DAFF. Where appropriate, other agencies can provide Liaison Officers to the IMT. If responding to 
more than one incident, DAFF can establish multiple IMTs. 
Agriculture Crisis Coordination Centre (AgriCCC) 
The AgriCCC function is a virtual conduit between DAFF and the NSR. The AgriCCC shares situational 
awareness between agencies, coordinates general communication and facilitates agency representation for 
coordination mechanisms, such as the NCM. Situational awareness materials, including scientific, technical 
and operational information, will be shared with relevant Australian Government agencies, state and territory 
agriculture and biosecurity agencies and affected industries to maintain a common operating picture. 
Liaison Officers 
In addition to Liaison officers received from other agencies, DAFF can deploy Liaison Officers to other 
operations centres or agencies to facilitate shared situational awareness and response coordination. For 
example, DAFF can deploy Liaison Officers to the NSR, State Coordination Centres and other Australian 
Government agencies.

6.8 
Crisis communication 
To ensure consistent messaging across government during AUSBIOAGPLAN activation, the Lead 
Coordinating Senior Official, supported by DAFF, is responsible for coordinating an Australian Government 
crisis communication strategy and disseminating of whole-of-Australian Government talking points, key 
messages, media releases and public information. 
During a biosecurity crisis DAFF will: 
• Develop a crisis communications strategy in line with the relevant Australian Government Crisis 
Communication Guidelines. 
• Develop and disseminate national talking points. 
• Coordinate crisis communication key messages and products with relevant states and territories. 
• Confirm key spokespeople and tempo of engagement. 
• Liaise with media and public information officers across government, and other stakeholders including 
media outlets as required. 
• Anticipate and resolve communication risks, including misinformation and disinformation. 
• Incorporate and prioritise coordinated and consistent key messages across government. 
• Adjust communications as the event, the media cycle and community needs evolve. 
• Disseminate crisis communication products across the Australian Government, state and territory 
governments, industry and other stakeholders as required or deemed relevant. 
• Support other areas of government that are organising media conferences. 
National Biosecurity Communication and Engagement Network 
The National Biosecurity Communication and Engagement Network (NBCEN) supports the national 
biosecurity response arrangements and advises the CC and NMG on best practice crisis communication. It 
provides a forum to support unity of effort and maintain national consistency of communication and 
engagement efforts for biosecurity events. 
NBCEN comprises communication managers from Australian and state and territory agencies responsible for 
biosecurity, Animal Health Australia, and Plant Health Australia. NBCEN can also include communication 
managers from affected industries, other agencies or organisations critical to the response. 
NBCEN will engage across governments, and with industry and stakeholders such as media outlets, to 
ensure relevant information is provided to the public in a timely manner. 
6.9. Resourcing and surge 
The Lead Coordinating Senior Official may co-opt people and resources internally as appropriate to resolve 
the crisis. A prolonged biosecurity crisis or a crisis of significant scale or complexity could erode DAFF’s 
capacity to respond to a crisis event. When internal surge options are exhausted, DAFF may use surge 
workforce options across the Australian Public Service to augment its coordination arrangements. These 
options may include the surge reserve maintained by the Australian Public Service Commission (APSC), 
which mobilises staff in large numbers to respond to significant crises when required.

7. 
Requests for assistance 
7.1. Domestic 
A domestic biosecurity or agricultural crisis may require Australian Government resources to support the 
impacted jurisdiction.  
The National Biosecurity Response Team (NBRT) is a group of trained and experienced personnel that may 
be deployed to assist a jurisdiction in the response to biosecurity incidents. NBRT members are personnel 
from government agencies with knowledge, experience and training in responding to biosecurity incidents. 
NBRT includes a functional cohort (personnel for working in a response team) and a more experienced 
mentor cohort (which can assist in setting up an initial response structure and provide guidance to less 
experienced response personnel). Deployment is managed using the Interstate Deployment Arrangements 
for Biosecurity Responses. 
The Interstate Deployment Arrangements (IDA) for Biosecurity Responses are national arrangements to 
facilitate the sharing of non-financial resources between biosecurity agencies. DAFF may provide a national 
coordination function under these arrangements. 
DAFF as the Australian Government Coordinating Agency will maintain contact with impacted jurisdictions to 
understand the resourcing needed to prepare for, respond to, and recover from the crisis. If additional non-
financial assistance may be required, or a Request for Assistance (RFA) is anticipated, contact should be 
made with DAFF as the responsible Australian Government Coordinating Agency. 
If additional non-financial assistance may be required, or a Request for Assistance (RFA) is anticipated 
through existing arrangements in the Australian Government Disaster Response Plan (COMDISPLAN), 
contact should be made with DAFF as the Australian Government Coordinating Agency. For further 
information on the provision of non-financial assistance to states and territories, see COMDISPLAN. 
7.2. International 
During a domestic biosecurity or agricultural crisis, international non-financial assistance may be required or 
offered to Australia to assist with response or recovery efforts. Offers of international assistance, or requests 
for international assistance, and coordinating the reception of international assistance is covered by 
arrangements in the interim Australian Government Reception of International Assistance Plan 
(AUSRIAPLAN).  
The International Animal Health Emergency Reserve (IAHER) is a formal arrangement that provides 
participating countries access to additional human resources in the event of an emergency animal disease 
outbreak. Australia, Canada, Ireland, New Zealand, United Kingdom and United States of America are 
signatories. 
8. 
Financial arrangements 
Agencies under AUSBIOAGPLAN are responsible for their own expenses consistent with their 
responsibilities. If assistance in the form of personnel or physical resources is provided across agencies or 
jurisdictions, financial considerations are managed according to the arrangements under which the 
assistance is provided.
