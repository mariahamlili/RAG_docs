To date, there has been no commercial release of GM wheat in Australia, nor has there been
any application for commercial release submitted to the regulator.
¢
GM wheat is not grown in Australia for commercial purposes.
-
However, there are GM wheat field trials running in Australia, managed and
regulated by one of the most rigorous frameworks in the world.
{
o
Since 2005, the regulator has approved eleven small-GM wheat field trials,
each limited in size and duration.
|
-
GM wheat from these trials is not permitted to enter the commercial human or animal
‘
food supply.
;
;
|
e
The development and use of genetically modified organisms in Australia is regulated through
;
,
an integrated framework that includes the Gene Technology Act 2000 and corresponding state
i
and territory legislation.
,
-—
The Gene Technology Act 2000, administered by the Gene Technology Regulator
(www.ogtr.gov.au), underpins the national scheme for regulating genetically
:
modified organisms (GMOs) and has as its focus the protection of human health and
|
the environment.
-
Economic and marketing considerations such as coexistence and segregation in
agricultural supply chains are addressed through state-specific requirements or
,
industry protocols.
|
e
Further information on Australian GM wheat trials, including the comprehensive Risk
|
Assessments and Risk Management Plans, the location of each field trial and applications
;
under consideration can be found on the Office of Gene Technology Regulation website at
i
www.ogtr.gov.au. The regulator conducts a rigorous science-based risk assessment and
extensive public and expert consultation for each trial they approve.
