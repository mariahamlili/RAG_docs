Policy Decision 25-02 
Testing arrangements and cost sharing of diagnostic testing 
expenses during an emergency animal disease response 
Animal Health Committee 
Purpose 
This policy has been developed to clarify arrangements in an emergency animal disease (EAD) 
response for: 
• diagnostic testing of samples relating to the outbreak,  
• invoicing procedures for samples submitted to the Australian Centre for Disease Preparedness 
(ACDP), and  
• eligibility of diagnostic testing expenses incurred by infected and non-infected jurisdictions for 
cost-sharing. 
Scope 
This policy applies to samples submitted under an approved Emergency Animal Disease Response 
Plan (EADRP).  
Exclusion testing by ACDP 
ACDP does not charge jurisdictions for the costs of initial testing of samples from the first suspected 
premises to confirm or exclude an EAD outbreak. This testing is funded by ACDP as part of their 
normal commitments. 
EAD surveillance plans  
When an EAD outbreak has been confirmed, infected jurisdictions develop surveillance plans that 
set out what samples will be collected and submitted for laboratory testing.  
Non-infected jurisdictions that anticipate having to conduct additional surveillance for the EAD 
above routine exclusion testing / normal commitments may also develop surveillance plans for the 
EAD. If there is more than one non-infected jurisdiction performing additional surveillance, the 
development of a national surveillance plan, led by the Australian Government Department of 
Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry, is preferred. 
Cost sharing of testing done in an EAD response 
An infected or non-infected jurisdiction that wishes to cost share expenses incurred for testing (by 
ACDP or jurisdictional laboratories etc.) of EAD samples during an EAD outbreak must submit an 
emergency animal disease response plan (EADRP) that details their proposed surveillance strategy

and associated budget to the Consultative Committee on Emergency Animal Diseases (CCEAD) and 
National Management Group (NMG). 
Costs incurred by a jurisdiction for activities conducted as part of an approved EADRP are eligible 
for cost sharing.  
Testing of samples at ACDP during an EAD response 
The following samples collected as part of an approved EADRP may be submitted by the infected or 
non-infected jurisdiction to ACDP for testing: 
• Any sample for which the jurisdictional laboratory does not have the capability or capacity to 
conduct definitive testing (using tests for which the laboratory has been validated under the 
LEADDR network). 
• Any sample that has tested positive at a jurisdictional laboratory. 
• Any sample where an inconclusive test result cannot be resolved by further testing at the 
jurisdictional laboratory. 
Charging for testing conducted by ACDP in an EAD response 
ACDP will: 
• charge jurisdictions for testing of all response surveillance samples relating to the EAD response 
collected post the initial investigation to confirm the EAD.  
• continue to charge for testing of response surveillance samples until the NMG declare that cost 
sharing of the response has ceased (e.g. the outbreak has been eradicated, a transition to 
management program has been completed or the cost shared response ceases). 
• not charge jurisdictions for any exclusion testing for other EADs, including such testing on 
samples collected for response surveillance. 
• also charge an additional fee for any staff overtime and other emergency management costs 
related to testing of EAD samples submitted as part of surveillance for an EAD response.  
• advise all CVOs of the rate to be charged for each test during the EAD response. 
• issue a monthly invoice to each jurisdiction who has submitted surveillance samples for the 
response. The invoice will detail the type of testing conducted, and the number and cost of each 
test conducted. 
The CVO or delegate of each jurisdiction will advise the ACDP of the email address/es to which 
invoices for EAD response testing costs are to be sent. 
Messaging should be simple and in language appropriate to meet stakeholder communication needs.
